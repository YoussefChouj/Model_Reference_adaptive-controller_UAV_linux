The Kalman Gain Your Mahony Filter Hard-Codes

You watched a Kalman filter explainer and took good notes. This lesson does two things: fixes the one bug in those notes, and shows you that your firmware already runs the Kalman filter's predict/correct loop at 1 kHz — with the gain frozen to a hand-tuned constant.

Source: "This One Equation Powers GPS, Rockets, and Robots" (your notes) · grounded in imu_update.c and the new reference card.

1 · Your notes, audited — one bug

Your summary is essentially right: fuse two Gaussians, the product is a new Gaussian that is more certain than either source, and the whole filter is that idea applied recursively. But one line has a typo that will bite you the day you tune a real filter:

The bug: you wrote "measurement equation: z = Hx + v (uncertainty covariance Q)". The measurement noise v has covariance R, not Q. There are two separate noise dials and they answer two different questions:
  • Q (process noise) — how much do I distrust my model between measurements? Inflates P during PREDICT.
  • R (measurement noise) — how much do I distrust this sensor? Weighs against P in the UPDATE gain.
Tuning a Kalman filter is choosing the Q:R ratio. Mix them up and every intuition inverts: raising Q makes the filter trust the sensor more (jumpier), raising R makes it trust the model more (smoother, laggier).

2 · The whole filter is one number

Everything in your screenshots collapses into the Kalman gain K = σ₁² / (σ₁² + σ₂²) — the fraction of the innovation (μ₂ − μ₁) you accept. Drag the two uncertainties and watch where the fused belief lands:

Three things to verify with your own hands before moving on:

  1. Make the measurement very confident (small σ₂) → K → 1, the fused mean snaps to the measurement.
  2. Make it garbage (large σ₂) → K → 0, the prediction barely moves. A Kalman filter never has to reject a sensor; a bad sensor simply stops mattering.
  3. No matter what you do, the fused σ is smaller than both sources — σ² = (1−K)σ₁². Even a terrible measurement shrinks uncertainty a little.

3 · You already run this loop at 1 kHz

Here is the payoff. Your firmware's attitude estimator — IMU_Update_Mahony() in imu_update.c, called at 1 kHz from IMU_DataDeal_Task — has exactly the predict/correct anatomy from your fourth screenshot:

Kalman filterYour Mahony filter
PREDICT x̂⁻ = A x̂  ·  P⁻ = APAᵀ + Q Propagate quaternion q̂ with the gyro rates ω
INNOVATE z − H x̂⁻ ωerr = a⃗meas × v̂down (accel "down" vs estimated "down")
GAIN Kk = P⁻HᵀS⁻¹ — recomputed every tick from P, Q, R Kp, Ki — constants you hand-tuned once
UPDATE x̂ = x̂⁻ + K(z − Hx̂⁻)  ·  P = (I−KH)P⁻ ωcorr = ω + Kp·ωerr + Ki·∫ωerr — no covariance kept

A fixed-gain complementary filter like Mahony's is, in the classical sense, a steady-state Kalman filter: if Q and R never change, Kk converges to a constant — so you can skip the covariance bookkeeping entirely and just ship the converged gain. That is a choice, and for your firmware it is a good one: at 1 kHz on an STM32F4 the covariance update is real CPU, and Mahony's cross-product form is provably stable on SO(3) where a linear KF is not (attitude isn't a vector space — see Mahony et al. 2008).

What the fixed gain gives up: the filter can't adapt its trust. During aggressive maneuvers the accelerometer measures thrust, not gravity — a Kalman filter would inflate R and lower K on its own; your Mahony trusts the accel exactly as much as it does in hover. And it's not just attitude: the SINS position corrections (SINS.h, the in_est/in_obs/fix_ki observer struct) are fixed-gain too. Your entire estimator stack is predict/correct with frozen gains — there is no Kalman filter anywhere in this firmware. Knowing precisely what that trade-off is, is the win of this lesson.
